## What each source covers (quick data map)

Use this to route MI questions to the right source. All studies are of the same
agent — NTLA-2002 (the development code), now named **lonvoguran ziclumeran
(lonvo-z)** — funded by Intellia Therapeutics.

### NEJM-2024-PH1 — Phase 1 dose-escalation (Longhurst et al.)
- **Design:** phase 1 dose-escalation portion of a combined phase 1–2 trial
  (NCT05120830); single IV dose 25 mg (n=3), 50 mg (n=4), 75 mg (n=3). Primary
  endpoints: safety / side-effect profile. Secondary/exploratory: PK, PD, clinical
  efficacy (investigator-confirmed attacks).
- **Safety:** most common AEs infusion-related reactions and fatigue; no
  dose-limiting toxic effects, no serious AEs, no grade ≥3 AEs, no clinically
  important lab findings.
- **PD (kallikrein):** mean change in total plasma kallikrein −67% (25 mg),
  −84% (50 mg), −95% (75 mg) from baseline to latest assessment.
- **Efficacy (exploratory):** mean change in monthly attack rate wk 1–16 was
  −91% (25 mg), −97% (50 mg), −80% (75 mg); across all patients −95% through
  latest assessment.

### NEJM-2024-PH2 — Phase 2 randomized (Cohn et al.) + NEJM-2024-PH2-SUPPL
- **Design:** phase 2 portion of the phase 1–2 trial (NCT05120830); randomized
  2:2:1 to single dose lonvo-z 25 mg (n=10), 50 mg (n=11), or placebo (n=6).
  Primary endpoint: monthly attack rate wk 1–16. Secondary: safety, PK, PD.
- **Efficacy:** estimated mean monthly attack rate 0.70 (95% CI 0.25–1.98) at
  25 mg, 0.65 (95% CI 0.24–1.76) at 50 mg, 2.82 (95% CI 0.80–9.89) placebo —
  −75% (25 mg) and −77% (50 mg) vs placebo. Attack-free wk 1–16: 40% (4/10) at
  25 mg, 73% (8/11) at 50 mg.
- **Safety:** most common AEs headache, fatigue, nasopharyngitis.
- **PD (kallikrein):** mean change in total plasma kallikrein −55% (25 mg),
  −86% (50 mg) to week 16; unchanged with placebo.
- **Supplement:** CONSORT diagram, PK of NTLA-2002 analytes, AE-QoL, responder
  status, and a phase 1 update.

### NEJM-2026-HAELO — Pivotal Phase 3 HAELO trial (Cohn et al.)
- **Design:** phase 3, double-blind (NCT06634420); randomized 2:1, patients ≥16 y
  with HAE with C1-inhibitor deficiency, single IV lonvo-z 50 mg (n=52) vs placebo
  (n=28), N=80. Primary endpoint: investigator-confirmed monthly attack rate
  wk 5–28. Median follow-up 7.5 months (range 4.9–12.8) as of 10 Feb 2026.
- **Baseline:** median age 41.5 y; 69% female; 92% C1INH type 1 / 8% type 2; 71%
  on long-term prophylaxis before enrollment; mean baseline attack rate 3.5/month
  in both groups.
- **Primary endpoint (attack rate wk 5–28):** LS-mean 0.26 (95% CI 0.15–0.45)
  lonvo-z vs 2.10 (95% CI 1.55–2.86) placebo; relative difference **−87%**
  (95% CI −93 to −78; **P<0.001**). Separation emerged within ~4 weeks of infusion.
- **Key secondary endpoints (all met, all P<0.001):**
  - Attacks requiring on-demand therapy: 0.19 (0.10–0.36) vs 1.79 (1.27–2.54);
    **−89%** (95% CI −94 to −79).
  - Moderate/severe attacks: 0.11 (0.06–0.23) vs 1.23 (0.84–1.81); **−91%**
    (95% CI −96 to −81).
  - Attack-free wk 5–28: **32/52 (62%)** lonvo-z vs 3/28 (11%) placebo; odds ratio
    12.81 (95% CI 3.45–47.55).
  - AE-QoL change from baseline to wk 28: −23.51 (95% CI −27.64 to −19.38) lonvo-z
    vs −6.47 (−12.26 to −0.68) placebo; difference −17.04 (95% CI −24.15 to −9.93);
    exceeds the 6-point minimal clinically important difference (lower = better).
- **Other endpoints:** ≥90% reduction in monthly attack rate in 39/52 (75%)
  lonvo-z vs 5/28 (18%) placebo; 60% (31/52) attack-free without long-term
  prophylaxis through last follow-up.

### AAAAI-2025-061 — Long-term durability & safety, 50 mg (Magerl et al., abstract)
**Evidence tier / caveats:** congress abstract (not a full peer-reviewed
manuscript); open-label, single-arm longer-term follow-up — no concurrent control.
Pools all patients who received lonvo-z 50 mg in the Phase 1/2 program
(NCT05120830), including former placebo and 25-mg patients who crossed over to
50 mg. Label as abstract data in any letter.
- **Population / follow-up:** N=28 received lonvo-z 50 mg (initial 50 mg n=15;
  25→50 mg n=7; placebo→50 mg n=6). As of 12 Feb 2025, median follow-up after
  50 mg was 18.9 months (range 10.2–24.4) for the initial-50 mg cohort — i.e.
  **up to ~2 years** — 5.6 months (PBO→50 mg) and 2.8 months (25→50 mg).
- **Durability of efficacy:** mean (SD) monthly attack-rate reduction from baseline
  to latest assessment **95% (13.2)** without long-term prophylaxis (LTP). At latest
  follow-up, **93% (26/28) were attack-free and LTP-free** for a median 10.2 months
  (range 0.5–24.0).
- **Safety:** most common AEs (≥20%) infusion-related reaction 50%, fatigue 32%,
  headache 21%, upper respiratory tract infection 21%; all grade 1/2 and generally
  transient. Safety of crossover cohorts consistent with the initial 50-mg cohort.
- **Conclusion (authors):** with up to 2 years of follow-up, a one-time lonvo-z
  50 mg treatment led to robust, sustained reductions in HAE attacks without the
  need for LTP, with an acceptable safety profile.
- **Safety:** on-treatment AEs in 92% (lonvo-z) vs 86% (placebo) through wk 28.
  TEAEs >10% in lonvo-z (lonvo-z; placebo): infusion-related reaction (62%; 18%),
  headache (19%; 11%), fatigue (14%; 11%), nasopharyngitis (14%; 32%), back pain
  (12%; 11%), upper respiratory tract infection (12%; 7%). **All AEs grade 1–2; no
  grade ≥3 and no serious AEs in the lonvo-z group.** All infusion-related
  reactions grade 1–2, related, mostly transient/resolved same day; commonest IRR
  symptoms headache, flushing, chest pain/tightness. Median lonvo-z infusion time
  3.9 h (range 2.5–5.3).
