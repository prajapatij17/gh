SUPPLEMENTARY METHODS
Screening
At screening, patients were asked, “What was your sex (at birth)?” Options were male or female. No 
patients identified as Hispanic or Latino. Patients were asked, “Do you consider yourself Hispanic/Latino 
or not Hispanic/Latino?” Options were Hispanic/Latino; not Hispanic/Latino; not reported; unknown. 
Twenty-six patients identified as White or Caucasian, and one patient identified as Native Hawaiian or 
other Pacific Islander. Patients were asked, “Which of the following designations best describe you (more
than one choice is acceptable)?” Options were American Indian or Alaska Native; Asian; Black or 
African American; Native Hawaiian or other Pacific Islander; White or Caucasian; not reported; 
unknown.
Concomitant Medications
Patients were permitted to use on-demand medications to treat angioedema attacks during the study. The 
following medications were prohibited during the screening and 16-week primary observation period: 
kallikrein B1 (KLKB1) targeted therapies, including antisense oligonucleotides, prekallikrein, and/or 
kallikrein-lowering therapies; ecallantide; attenuated androgens and berotralstat; lanadelumab; anti￾fibrinolytics; any other therapy intended for long-term prophylaxis (prevention) of hereditary angioedema 
(HAE) attacks; chronic antithrombotic therapy; ACE inhibitors; and estrogen-containing medications with 
systemic absorption. Following the 16-week primary observation period, patients were permitted to 
initiate other therapy to manage their HAE at the discretion of the investigator.
Supplementary Statistical Analysis 
Pharmacodynamic analyses included all patients in the safety set in whom the total plasma kallikrein 
protein level was measured at least once before and at least once after the administration of study drug. 
Pharmacokinetic analyses included patients in the safety set who had at least one blood sample in which 
concentrations of any of the four plasma analytes of NTLA-2002 could be evaluated. Patient-reported 
outcome end point analyses were performed in the full analysis set. For angioedema attack–related end 
points, the 95% CIs were reported for model-based estimates, mean and standard error were presented for 
the descriptive summary; for all other end points, descriptive statistics (n, mean, median, standard 
deviation, minimum, maximum) were used in summarizing the data.
The primary end point of number of angioedema attacks per month during the 16-week primary 
observation period was compared between NTLA-2002 and placebo using a negative binominal 
regression model adjusting for baseline attack rate. The model included the treatment arm and the 
baseline attack rate as independent variables. The logarithm of follow-up duration (in months) for each 
patient during the primary observation period was included in the model as an offset variable to adjust for 
differences in follow-up time. From this model, the least squares mean attack rate and the corresponding 
95% CI for each treatment arm as well as the mean rate ratio and the corresponding 95% CI for 
NTLA-2002 relative to placebo were estimated. These estimates were reported as mean angioedema 
attack rates per month by transforming the estimates using the exponential function. Missing data were 
assumed to be missing at random in this analysis. Patients who did not have a week 16 assessment at the 
time of the analysis had the time-adjusted attack rate included in the analysis. Pearson Chi-square test was 
performed to assess goodness of fit of the model, and the dispersion coefficient estimate from the model 
was examined for overdispersion of the data.
The angioedema attack–related secondary end points were analyzed using the same model approach 
as the primary end point. The angioedema attack rate and percentage change from baseline were also

SUPPLEMENTARY RESULTS
Pharmacokinetics
Following NTLA-2002 administration, all four analytes demonstrated a dose-proportional plasma 
exposure (Fig. S4). NTLA-2002 analytes rapidly declined from peak concentrations, followed by a 
secondary peak at approximately 12 hours post infusion, and then a log-linear elimination phase. All 
analytes, except DMG-PEG2k (biodegradable by design)1 were quantifiable in plasma up to 29 days post
infusion. DMG-PEG2k declined to below quantification limits within approximately 24 hours post end of 
infusion.
Phase 1 Update
Baseline characteristics were reported previously.2 In brief, the median age was 51 (range, 26 to 73) 
years, 60% were male, and the median number of angioedema attacks during the 90 days before screening 
was 6 (range, 3 to 54).
Between the end of the primary 16-week observation period and the latest assessment (week 104, 
25 mg; week 72, 50 mg; week 88, 75 mg) the mean percentage change from baseline in monthly 
angioedema attack rate was −99%, −100%, and −100% in the 25-mg, 50-mg, and 75-mg arms (Table S5,
Fig. S6). The mean percentage change in monthly angioedema attack rate between baseline and the latest 
assessment was −97%, −100%, and −97% in the 25-mg, 50-mg, and 75-mg arms. Eight patients remain 
attack-free since the end of the primary observation period (Fig. S7). None of the six patients who 
stopped their long-term prophylaxis after administration of NTLA-2002 have resumed, and five reported 
no attacks since withdrawal. 
NTLA-2002-treated patients continued to show a low rate of adverse events (AEs) (Table S6). No 
dose-limiting toxic effects, serious AEs, or AEs of grade 3 or higher occurred after administration of 
NTLA-2002. The most common AEs were infusion-related reactions (70%), COVID-19 (60%), fatigue 
(60%), and upper respiratory tract infection (50%). 
Dose-dependent reductions in the total plasma kallikrein protein levels were sustained, with a mean 
(SD) percentage change from baseline to the latest assessment of −70% (21.4), −88% (6.8), and −95% 
(1.1) in the 25-mg, 50-mg, and 75-mg arms, respectively (Fig. S8).

Table S1. Representativeness of Study Patients.
Category 
Disease under 
investigation 
Hereditary angioedema due to C1INH deficiency (HAE-C1INH)
Special considerations related to: 
Sex and gender Males and females are affected equally at the genetic level. Symptoms are sometimes more 
severe in female patients, possibly due to fluctuation in female sex hormones, and this may 
contribute to a slightly higher likelihood of diagnosis. 
Age HAE is an autosomal dominant genetic disease; therefore, all patients who are affected have 
the disease from birth. The age at which attacks begin is variable, but in most patients, the 
disease presents in childhood or adolescence. Patients will continue to have attacks throughout 
their lifetime. 
Race or ethnic 
group 
There are no known differences in prevalence, disease pathophysiology, or responses to 
treatment among racial or ethnic groups. 
Geography There are no known differences in prevalence in different countries. However, HAE is a rare 
disease (1:50,000 population) but shares symptoms with other more common diseases. This 
may contribute to patients being misdiagnosed or underdiagnosed in developing countries or 
countries that lack access to diagnostic tests and evidence-based therapies. 
Other considerations There are two main types of HAE: HAE-C1INH-Type 1 (≈85% of patients) and HAE-C1INH￾Type 2 (≈15% of patients). Attacks most often affect the skin, gastrointestinal tract, and upper 
airway. The frequency and severity of attacks is highly variable across patients. 
Overall 
representativeness 
of this study
This was a small phase 2 study of 27 adult patients with HAE-C1INH. The age distribution of 
study patients was consistent with what is observed in adults with HAE-C1INH. There was a 
slightly higher number of men than women. Participants were representative of patient 
populations in countries with study sites, which have predominantly White or Caucasian 
populations: Australia, France, Germany, the Netherlands, New Zealand, and the United 
Kingdom. The number of patients with HAE-C1INH-Type 1 and HAE-C1INH-Type 2 disease 
was representative of the overall HAE-C1INH population. Patients also had varying types, 
frequency, and severity of HAE attacks, which is typical in patients with HAE.
